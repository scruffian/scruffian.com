- Saturday, March 13, 2004


Killer Sheep by Abi Varley
It was a pleasant Saturday in March, and Rog (with Ez in carrier), Abi, Ben and Bek were sitting enjoying a drink halfway through a walk through mellow Warwickshire. Having drunk up the intrepid five went on their way, following instructions to go over a stile and into a field through which they were to walk. An initial glance showed that the field was fairly fullof sheep, docile herbivores it was presumed, and a few small lambs, so over they went.
Almost immediately it became apparent that the first impression made by the sheep was not to be believed, they were overly confident, in fact quite pushy, and very inquisitive. Some fast thinking attributed this to the sandwiches which were being carried, and these were hurriedly replaced in bags and pockets. (Comment here that when Bek's sandwich emerged from her pocket it had been squashed and disintegrated into breadcrubs, such was the terror which had been experienced!). Anyway, the removal from sight of the sandwiches did absolutely nothing to prevent the sheep running their very fastest from all corners of the field towards the group of walkers, and then forming a very disorderly jostling group around and behind them. The close proximity and jostling ness of these sheep (not to mention their rolling eyes, fangs and razor sharp claws) terrified Abi and Bek, who clung to their respective men begging them to doooo something. Rog, as the older leader of the pack instructed everyone to keep walking and stop being silly, sothey did, keep walking at least, but the sheep were keeping walking too, so this didn't much help, and the end of the field didn't seem much closer. Ez thought the whole thing very funny from his high up safe out of sheep's way vantage point. To cut a long and panicked walk across the field being harassed by at least 379 sheep short, in the nick of time Ben pretended to be a dragon, spread out his wings and ran at them breathing fire, and they backed of a bit, giving the girls time to run for the gate, which turned out to be rusty and high, but which they scaled with exceptional agility never before shown. Leaving Ben to jog to the gate, and Rog to wonder up and down the filed wondering if it was the right one anyway. What should have been done became clear soon enough, when a photo of the criminal sheep was taken they all ran off - very camera shy.
We suggest you mark this walk in your book of such activities as 'very dangerous'!!
